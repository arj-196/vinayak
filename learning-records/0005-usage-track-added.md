# Track B added: usage limits as an enabling constraint, not a new mission

Arjun asked on 2026-09-09 for a course on token management, the five-hour window, and context
management. Confirmed with him: it is **for Vinayak, inside the existing journey**, grounded in
**the Claude app and Cowork** (not Claude Code), and the driving pain is **hitting limits
mid-work**.

**This is deliberately not a second mission.** The workspace holds one mission — portable master
processes — and this material serves it rather than competing with it. His own success criterion
is that a process runs to completion without him; a Cowork run that dies halfway because the
session allowance ran out fails that criterion for a reason that has nothing to do with how good
the process is. So usage is recorded as a constraint in [MISSION.md](../MISSION.md), with one
success criterion added, and the lessons are labelled **Track B** to keep them distinguishable
from the main path.

**Placement is the interesting decision.** The obvious move — three lessons in a block before
Cowork — is bad teaching, because a budget means nothing to someone who has never felt the
constraint. Instead the track is interleaved:

- **B1 now**, deliberately before anything expensive: find the meter, record a baseline from
  ordinary chat use. Cheap, protective, and it captures a *before* reading that only exists if
  taken now.
- **B2 immediately after his first Cowork run**, when he can compare the meter against a job he
  actually remembers. That is the tightest available feedback loop, and it is only available in
  that one window.
- **B3 after that**, on making a task fit — which is the lesson with real behaviour change in it
  and needs the felt experience of B2 underneath it.

**A useful interleave fell out of this.** Progressive disclosure — the reason a Skill costs
almost nothing until it fires, taught in L01 — is *the same mechanism* as context economy. B3
can therefore revisit Stage 01 material from a completely different angle rather than
introducing a new idea. Genuine interleaving, not manufactured.

**One thing to stay honest about.** No Anthropic document states when a five-hour session
actually begins or whether sessions roll. Do not fill that gap with a plausible-sounding rule.
B1 teaches reading the live countdown in Settings → Usage instead — more accurate, and a better
habit than a memorised mechanic.

## Track B written in full, 2026-09-09

B1–B3 now exist, plus [reference/usage-card.html](../reference/usage-card.html).

Two design notes worth keeping. **B2 is deliberately inverted** — practice before explanation —
because the meter reading is only comparable while the Cowork run is the last significant thing
he did; reading it a week later measures everything since. **B3's drill reuses the same four
lever options across all five scenarios**, which gives perfect answer-length parity for free and
turns the exercise into classification rather than recall of wording. The discriminating pair is
scenario 2 against scenario 3: a *document* you return to is a Project, a *process* you
re-explain is a Skill. That distinction is the one thing in Track B most likely to be got wrong
in the wild.

`assets/budget.js` was built rather than a worked example on the page, because the useful output
— which clock binds first — depends on his own two numbers and flips direction depending on them.
