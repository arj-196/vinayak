The line to pay attention to is which clock binds first. Most people assume the weekly limit is the one to fear, because it sounds bigger. The day you're actually blocked

Being stopped by one clock is a completely different situation from being stopped by the other, and the right response is different too.

Session limit hit

Wait it out

Resets every five hours, and the countdown is on your usage screen. Switch to ordinary chat, which costs a fraction of Cowork, or go and do something else. This is the good outcome.

Weekly limit hit

Plan differently

Applies across all models and refills only at your fixed weekly slot. ¹ Nothing you do speeds this up. The fix is upstream: spread big runs out rather than stacking them.

The escape hatch, and why it's not the plan

Pro accounts can turn on usage credits: instead of stopping, work continues on pay-as-you-go pricing at standard API rates, with a spending cap you set. ²

Genuinely useful when a deadline is real. But notice what it does — it converts a planning problem into a billing problem. If credits are how you get through a normal week, the job is too big for how you are running it, and that is what B3 is about.

API rates

The per-use price Anthropic charges developers, billed by the token rather than by the month. Credits bill at those rates, so the cost depends on the model — Haiku cheapest, Opus dearest.

You've hit the session limit. What happens next?

A few hours — the session resets every five, and your usage screen shows the countdown. This is the mild failure. Ordinary chat costs a fraction of Cowork, so it is often still usable while you wait.

You hit a limit with usage credits switched on. Then?

It continues on pay-as-you-go at standard API rates, within a cap you set. Worth having enabled for the week something genuinely cannot wait — but if you lean on it routinely, that is a signal to resize the job rather than to keep paying.

Before kicking off a long Cowork job, check what?

Time left in the session. Starting a forty-minute job with twenty minutes of session left is the single most avoidable way to lose work — and it takes five seconds to check.
