STAGE 01

Skills — writing the process down

Unlocked · Pro

Underlined titles are written and ready — the rest arrive as you get to them. A Skill is a master process in a plain text file. Claude picks it up on its own when it is relevant, and because it is just text, you can give it to someone.

●L01
Your first Skill, by talking
You describe a process out loud, Claude writes the file. No file editing at all. Win: a Skill that fires without you naming it.
15 min
○L02
Why it didn't fire
One field decides whether Claude ever reaches for your Skill. You'll break yours deliberately, then fix it. Win: you can debug a silent Skill.
12 min
○L03
Inside the file — and what really transfers
Markdown and YAML, learned by reading the Skill you wrote. Also the straight answer on "all models": what carries across and what doesn't. Win: the file stops looking like code.
15 min
○L04
Hand it to someone else
The centre of this whole course. Give your Skill to a colleague, watch where they get different output, and fix the Skill rather than coaching the person. Win: a process that works without you in the room.
15 min

STAGE 02

Reach — Claude touching your real work

Check: Microsoft tenant

A process that needs you to copy-paste the inputs isn't implemented yet. This stage removes the copy-pasting.

○L05
Connect Microsoft 365, run one errand
One connector, then a real errand across Outlook, Teams or SharePoint. Win: Claude reads your actual work.
Caveat we'll test first: the connector needs a Microsoft business tenant, and if yours is your employer's, their IT may have to allow it. The lesson has a no-connector fallback so you're never stuck.

Where your files actually live
You think in SharePoint sites and shared links. Cowork thinks in folders. This is the translation between the two — not a beginners' lesson, a genuinely confusing mismatch. Win: you can point Claude at anything, first time.

STAGE 03

Cowork — the process running itself

Unlocked · you can already see it

"Implemented where the code can work" — this is that stage. Cowork plans and executes multi-step jobs on real files instead of advising you.

○L07
First run, in a folder that doesn't matter
A small reversible job in a throwaway folder, so you meet permission prompts with nothing at stake. Covers Manual / Auto / Skip and what Cowork costs in usage. Win: you've watched an agent work and chosen its leash.
15 min
○B2
What that actually costsame day as L07
Read the meter against a job you still remember, and see the gap between chat and agent work in your own numbers. Also what to do the day you are blocked. Win: Cowork's price stops being a mystery.
Timing matters: this only works while L07 is fresh. Don't leave it a week.
10 min
○B3
Making a task fit
Why a long conversation costs more with every message, and the four levers that shrink a job: scope, fresh chats, Project caching, and — back to L01 — Skills. Win: you can size a job to the clock.
12 min
○L08
Your real deliverable, handed over
The recurring deck or spreadsheet, produced end-to-end by Cowork, steered by the Skills you wrote in Stage 01. Win: the real thing, done without you.
15 min
○L09
Make it repeat — and give it away
Put L08 on a schedule, then package the whole thing so a colleague can run it too. Closes the loop back to L04. Win: a master process, transferred and implemented.

STAGE 04

Claude Code — parked, on purpose

Not scheduled

Three more lessons exist here — the terminal, Skills living on your own machine, and subagents. They are deliberately not in your nine.

Why parked. You were offered "comfortable in the terminal" as your goal and chose to write your own answer instead — so it isn't what you're after. At under thirty minutes a week it would cost roughly two months that Stages 01–03 will spend better. You've already typed a terminal command, so this is a scheduling call, not a judgement about difficulty.

Say the word and it unlocks. The likely trigger: somewhere around L08 you'll want a process to run on files Cowork can't reach, or to run on a machine rather than in an app. That's the moment this stops being optional. Ask then.

Two things that will feel like detours

L04 and L09 make you go back. L04 sends you into a Skill you wrote three weeks earlier; L09 returns to L04's handover problem. Both will feel like repetition and both are the reason any of it is still with you in six months. Retrieving something you half remember is what makes it stick — re-reading it does almost nothing. The lessons marked recall above are where that happens on purpose.

One thing still missing

You left the "task you'd most like to stop doing by hand" blank. No problem — L01 asks you to pick one anyway, so whatever you choose there becomes the thread through L08 and the finish. Just tell us what you picked.
