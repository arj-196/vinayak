Stage 01 · Skills · Lesson 01

Your first Skill, by talking

You will not open a file in this lesson. You are going to describe something you already know how to do, and end up with a Skill that Claude reaches for on its own.

~15 min Pro — you're set No files, no terminal
Readone screen

The whole idea, in four sentences

You are already good at chat. The cost of being good at chat is that you re-supply the same context every time — your format, your rules, the four steps you always take.

A Skill is that context, written down once, that Claude loads by itself when it becomes relevant. Anthropic's own framing is the useful one: "Building a skill for an agent is like putting together an onboarding guide for a new hire." ¹

The part that makes it cheap: Claude does not read your Skills. It reads only their names and descriptions — and opens the full instructions only for the one that matches what you just asked for. ² Twenty Skills cost you almost nothing until one of them is needed.

description field

Every Skill carries a one-line summary of what it does and when to use it. That line is the only thing Claude sees when choosing. It is the whole game, and it is why the next lesson exists.

You do not need to know what a file, a folder, YAML or Markdown is to finish this lesson. Claude writes all of that for you today. You will meet those words in L03, by reading the file you are about to create — which is a much gentler introduction than being taught them cold.

Build one

Step 1 · Pick the task

Something you have done more than twice this month, that has rules living only in your head. From your world: the way you structure a client deck, how you clean an Excel sheet before it goes out, the shape of your Teams status update, how you turn meeting notes into an Outlook summary.

Do not pick something ambitious. Pick something small and slightly boring. Small and boring is the correct difficulty for a first Skill.

  1. Start a normal chat

    Open the desktop app. New conversation, normal chat — not Cowork yet.

  2. Paste this, filled in

    Replace the bracket with your task. Then send it.

    I want to create a Skill that captures how I do [YOUR TASK].

Interview me about it first — ask me one question at a time
about my steps, my format, and the rules I apply without
thinking. Don't write the Skill until you've asked at least
five questions and I've said we're done.

    The interview is the point. It drags out the rules you don't know you have — which are exactly the rules worth writing down.

  3. Answer honestly, including the messy bits

    "It depends" is a real answer — say what it depends on. Those conditions are the most valuable thing in the finished Skill.

  4. Ask for the Skill

    When the questions stop being useful, say:

    Good, that's everything. Now create the Skill.

    Claude knows this format natively — it will produce a properly structured Skill without you specifying anything about how one is built. ³

  5. Install it

    Go to Settings → Customize → Skills, then use + to add the one Claude just made. ⁴

    Claude may offer to hand you the file directly instead — either route is fine. If you get stuck here, ask; this screen has moved before.

  6. The real test

    Open a brand new conversation and ask for the task the way you normally would — without mentioning the Skill, and without naming it.

    That is the whole exercise. A Skill you have to summon by name has saved you nothing — and more importantly, it can't be handed to a colleague who doesn't know its name. The win is Claude reaching for it unprompted. Watch for a line in its response saying a Skill was used.
Tell us which task you picked. You left that question blank in the setup check, and whatever you choose here becomes the thread running through L08 and the finish line — the thing Cowork eventually produces without you. One line is enough.

If it didn't fire

Good — genuinely. That is the most common outcome and it is the entire subject of L02. Don't fix it yet, and don't rewrite it. Bring it to the next lesson as-is; a broken example you made yourself is worth more than a working one I hand you.

Three from memory

Answer before you scroll back. Getting one wrong from memory teaches you more than getting three right by re-reading — that discomfort is the mechanism, not a sign you failed.

Before Claude picks a Skill, what has it actually loaded?

Names and descriptions only. Everything else stays unread until that Skill is chosen — which is why having many Skills costs you almost nothing, and why the description carries all the weight.

Where do you manage custom Skills in the Claude app?

Settings → Customize → Skills. Worth remembering, because it's also where you go to check whether a Skill is switched on at all — a surprisingly common reason one seems broken.

Which mental model fits a Skill best?

An onboarding guide. The difference from a saved prompt matters: a saved prompt waits for you to fetch it, while a Skill is picked up on its own when the situation calls for it — and it can carry extra files, the way a real onboarding pack has appendices.
