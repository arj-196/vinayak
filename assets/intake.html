Before we start

Nine questions, then we build you a course

Arjun asked me to put together a hands-on course that gets you past chat and into Skills, Cowork and Claude Code. To pitch it at the right level I need to know what you actually have installed and what you have actually touched.

~4 minutes Mostly clicking Saves as you go
One rule: "never heard of it" is the single most useful answer you can give me. Every honest no here removes something confusing from your course. Guessing upward just gets you lessons that skip the part you needed.

And if none of the options describe you, don't force one — every question has an open line at the bottom. Typing there counts as answering it.
01

Which Claude plan are you on?

Check at claude.ai → your initials, bottom left → Settings → Billing. This one matters: it decides whether Cowork is available to you at all.

02

Where do you actually use Claude?

03

Open Claude and look at the box where you type. Do you see a Cowork option?

Look for a small toggle, tab or button labelled Cowork near the send button. Please actually look rather than guessing — this is the fastest way for me to know what to plan.

04

Skills — where are you with them?

They live at Settings → Customize → Skills. Worth opening that page now even if the answer is "never seen it".

05

Tick anything you've genuinely done before

pick any

This is the important one. It sets how much I explain versus how much I assume, so under-ticking costs you nothing and over-ticking costs you a confusing lesson. Leaving most of these blank is completely normal and expected.

06

Which of these do you live in during a normal week?

pick any

Claude can connect to several of these directly. Knowing which ones tells me what to wire up in the connectors lesson.

07

What's the one repetitive task you'd most like to stop doing by hand?

Two or three lines is plenty. Be boringly specific — "every Monday I pull numbers out of four spreadsheets and rewrite the same status summary" beats "reporting". Whatever you put here becomes the thing we actually build across the whole course, so pick something real and slightly annoying.

08

09

Realistically, how much time per week?

Answer with the number you'll actually hit on a bad week, not a good one. I'd rather build six small lessons you finish than three big ones you don't.

+

Optional: anything you've already tried that went badly?

A Skill that never fired, Cowork doing something alarming, a command that errored. Failures are the most useful thing you can hand me — they point straight at the lesson you need.
